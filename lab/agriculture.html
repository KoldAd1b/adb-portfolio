When Growing Food Is No Longer Enough: A Study of Indigenous Food Security in Bangladesh

A. Adib, N. Chakma, A. L. Ruano, P. F. da Silva, M. H. Bukhari, S. Chakma, A. Soltani, M. A. Islam, U. Haque

Agriculture & Food Security.

Summary

We ran focus groups with Indigenous communities of Bangladesh's Chittagong Hill Tracts. Participants told us their land had worn out, so they grow less of their food and buy more with cash-crop income. In seasonal crises, families sell what they own or borrow at high cost, and women carry more of the burden. We argued that food security depends on access to food as well as production.
